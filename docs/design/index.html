Weather-Aware Truck Routing

The request pipeline, the ETA mechanism, what a checkpoint carries, how a level is decided, and why the fastest route sometimes loses.

The one idea

Weather is scored at arrival, not at departure. A 299 mile route crosses six hours of forecast. Checkpoint 9 is not “weather now” — it is the weather at the hour the truck reaches mile 225. Everything else here follows from that one substitution.

Rehearse in three hours

  1. 0:00–0:50Say the story without the screen. Two places in, three routes out, each scored at its own arrival hour, ranked by severe then high then average then time. If you can say that in forty seconds without notes, the rest is detail.
  2. 0:50–1:40Own the risk module. Open risk.py and defend two choices: why the buckets are half-open, and why the load rules run after classification instead of inside it. Then name one test that would fail if either changed.
  3. 1:40–2:30Drive the app, not the slides. One live plan, one checkpoint popup, one slider sweep, one weight change from 38,000 to 52,000 lb. Rehearse the weight change until the numbers are automatic — it is the strongest thirty seconds you have.
  4. 2:30–3:00Record two takes. The first is throwaway. Watch it at 1.5× only for pacing and filler words, then record the keeper.
